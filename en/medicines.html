💊 Understand my medicines

Human insulin: confirm the formulation

This name alone cannot distinguish regular, NPH or mixed formulations. Only general insulin information is shown. Confirm the product, formulation and concentration with a pharmacist. Do not infer timing or duration from this result.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Humulin N (insulin isophane human) prescribing information (US)
Miglitol: use glucose for hypoglycemia

Prepare for hypoglycemia when miglitol is combined with insulin or secretagogues. Sucrose digestion is delayed: use glucose tablets or gel if awake and able to swallow. Do not give anything by mouth if unconscious or unable to swallow; call emergency services.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Miglitol prescribing information (US)
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026
Semaglutide: eyes and formulation

Injectable semaglutide labeling includes a retinopathy complication warning. Review existing retinopathy and rapid glucose improvement, and report vision changes. Injectable and oral instructions and indications differ.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Ozempic (semaglutide injection) prescribing information (US)
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment: Standards of Care 2026
Alogliptin: review heart failure risk

The US label calls for benefit-risk review and symptom monitoring in people at risk of heart failure. Share any heart failure or kidney impairment history.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Alogliptin prescribing information (US)

Medicines requiring hypoglycemia preparation

Insulin, sulfonylurea or glinide is present. Reduced meals, activity and alcohol change risk; other glucose-lowering medicines can increase it. Review monitoring and rapid glucose plans.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment: Standards of Care 2026
Review an incretin combination

Combining a DPP-4 inhibitor with GLP-1 or GIP/GLP-1 therapy is generally not recommended because added benefit is limited. Check the prescription intent; do not stop it yourself.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment: Standards of Care 2026
Use glucose for low blood sugar

Acarbose with insulin or secretagogues can cause hypoglycemia. Since acarbose delays sucrose digestion, use glucose tablets or gel. Do not feed an unconscious person; call emergency services.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Acarbose prescribing information (US)
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026
Several ingredients in one class

Several ingredients share a class. Check whether the combination is intentional or duplicative. This flag does not establish a prescribing error.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment: Standards of Care 2026
Insulin plus TZD: review edema and heart failure

Review fluid retention and heart failure risk with this combination. Report sudden weight gain, leg swelling or breathlessness.

Repaglinide + gemfibrozil

The US label contraindicates this combination due to severe hypoglycemia concerns. Promptly confirm it with your prescriber or pharmacist. This tool does not set discontinuation or dose changes.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Repaglinide prescribing information (US)
Repaglinide + clopidogrel

The US label advises avoiding this combination. Increased exposure and hypoglycemia risk require clinician review.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Repaglinide prescribing information (US)
Saxagliptin: heart failure warning

People at elevated heart failure risk need individualized benefit-risk review. Report breathlessness, edema or rapid weight gain. Do not interpret this as equal risk for all DPP-4 ingredients.

Read the evidence
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Saxagliptin prescribing information (US)
Kidney function review

Kidney function changes risk or dosing for metformin, some DPP-4 medicines, insulin and secretagogues. Clinicians should review eGFR and product labels. This tool does not calculate doses.

  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Sitagliptin / metformin prescribing information (US)
Review a heart failure history

Review fluid retention and heart failure worsening with TZDs. SGLT2 benefit and fluid or blood pressure plans depend on ingredient and condition.

Fasting, dehydration and ketoacidosis plan

An SGLT2 ingredient is present. Agree in advance on surgery, fasting and serious illness plans. Severe vomiting, abdominal pain or rapid breathing needs prompt assessment even without very high glucose.
