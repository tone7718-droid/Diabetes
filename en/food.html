🥗 Food: what and how much to eat?

Learn portions and sustainable choices instead of a forbidden-food list.

✅ Remember these

  1. You do not need to give up rice or noodles. Start with a plate that is half non-starchy vegetables, one quarter protein and one quarter rice or noodles.
  2. Cut down on sugary drinks and fruit juice; eat whole fruit in suitable portions.
  3. Before making big changes to what you eat, agree on medicine and glucose-checking plans with your care team.

Start with a balanced meal

You do not have to remove all rice, noodles or bread. Combine nonstarchy vegetables, fish, tofu, beans or lean protein, and appropriate grain portions. The plate method is a starting tool, not a calculation of individual energy needs.

An action today

  • For Korean meals, plan rice portions with fish or tofu and vegetable sides. For Vietnamese meals, consider noodle portions, sweet sauces and drinks together.

Exceptions and cautions

Kidney disease, heart failure or digestive conditions may require changes to protein, sodium, potassium or fluids. More vegetables or water is not always better.

Carbohydrate quantity and quality

Whole grains, beans and whole fruit can be good choices, but large portions still raise glucose. Juice and sugary drinks make it easy to consume a lot of sugar. Prefer suitable portions of whole fruit; blending alone does not make every food forbidden.

An action today

  • Read serving size, total carbohydrate and sugars together. Sugar-free does not necessarily mean carbohydrate-free.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Meal order is an optional supporting strategy

Some studies find smaller post-meal glucose excursions when vegetables and protein come before carbohydrate. This does not replace portion or food choices; long-term HbA1c effects must be distinguished from short-term glucose changes. Differences in participants and meals prevent a single promised percentage.

Read the evidence
  • Systematic review Efficacy of a meal sequence in patients with type 2 diabetes: systematic review and meta-analysis
  • Systematic review The Impact of Macronutrient Ordering on Postprandial Glycaemic Control in Diabetes: A Systematic Review

Fasting, low-carbohydrate diets and medicines

Several patterns, including Mediterranean-style eating and appropriate carbohydrate reduction, can fit individual preferences and goals. Insulin, sulfonylurea or meglitinide users must consider hypoglycemia when intake falls. Extreme carbohydrate restriction or prolonged fasting with SGLT2 medicines can increase ketoacidosis concerns.

An action today

  • Agree on medication and monitoring plans before major dietary changes.
