🚶 Start walking and strength exercise safely

Start small and consider medicines and foot health.

✅ Remember these

  1. Start with a comfortable ten-minute walk after meals, and get up briefly about every 30 minutes when sitting for long periods.
  2. The goal is 150 minutes of moderate activity and strength exercise 2–3 times a week. Increase little by little from where you are now.
  3. If you use medicines that can cause low glucose, carry rapid glucose. Stop right away if you have chest pain, severe dizziness or trouble breathing.

Why activity helps

Muscle contraction supports glucose uptake through pathways partly separate from insulin signaling. Regular activity improves insulin sensitivity, fitness and function. Exercise complements food and medication; glucose does not always fall immediately.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Weekly goals and a starting dose

Many adults are advised to aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly and strength exercise on 2–3 nonconsecutive days. Spread aerobic activity over at least three days, with no more than two consecutive inactive days. Begin above your current activity level even if below the target.

An action today

  • Try ten minutes of comfortable walking after a meal, and interrupt long sitting about every 30 minutes.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Strength exercise at home

Choose safe movements such as chair sit-to-stand, wall push-ups, band rows or supported heel raises. Begin with few repetitions and sets, then increase once the movement is stable. Breathe normally and use a stable chair.

Exceptions and cautions

Discuss weight-bearing exercise with an active foot ulcer, and strenuous or straining exercise with proliferative retinopathy. Swimming is not automatically safe for an open foot wound.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Public patient education NIDDK · Diabetes & Foot Problems

Prepare according to medication risk

Insulin, sulfonylurea and meglitinide users should consider glucose before and after activity and delayed hypoglycemia. Pre-exercise carbohydrate needs depend on glucose, active insulin and activity duration. One threshold does not fit everyone.

An action today

  • Carry rapid glucose and agree on a monitoring plan for new activity. Stop and seek help for chest pain, severe dizziness or breathing difficulty.

Exceptions and cautions

Do not try to force glucose down through exercise when unwell with high glucose and ketones.
