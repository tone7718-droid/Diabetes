🩸 Diabetes: what happens inside the body?

Understand glucose, insulin and your test results.

✅ Remember these

  1. Blood glucose is the amount of glucose in your blood. Insulin helps the body use glucose.
  2. Diagnostic thresholds (fasting glucose 126 mg/dL or higher, HbA1c 6.5% or higher) are not your personal target. Set your target with your care team.
  3. Write down whether each test was fasting, after a meal or an oral glucose tolerance test.

Glucose and insulin

Blood glucose is the concentration of glucose in blood. Digestion supplies glucose, and the liver also releases glucose between meals. Insulin from the pancreas reduces liver glucose production and helps muscle and fat cells take up glucose.

Exceptions and cautions

Blood vessel damage is not caused by sticky sugar coating vessels. Prolonged high glucose and other metabolic and vascular factors contribute.

Type 1 and type 2 differ

Type 1 usually involves autoimmune damage to insulin-producing cells and requires insulin treatment. It can begin in adulthood. Type 2 involves insulin resistance and impaired insulin secretion, influenced by genetics, age, body fat and living conditions.

Exceptions and cautions

Other forms include gestational diabetes and diabetes related to pancreatic disease or medicines. This book focuses on adult prediabetes and type 2, not pregnancy or type 1 treatment.

Diagnosis is different from treatment targets

Laboratory fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL, 2-hour glucose 140–199 mg/dL after a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test, or HbA1c 5.7–6.4% are prediabetes ranges. Diabetes thresholds are 126, 200 and 6.5%, respectively. An oral glucose tolerance test is not an ordinary after-meal reading.

Without unequivocal symptoms or a hyperglycemic crisis, confirmation is usually needed. Anemia and other conditions may affect HbA1c. A clinician interprets the findings together.

An action today

  • Record whether the test was fasting, after a meal or an oral glucose tolerance test.

Exceptions and cautions

An HbA1c below 7% is considered for many adults, but national guidance, age and hypoglycemia risk affect the target. A diagnostic threshold is not automatically your target.
