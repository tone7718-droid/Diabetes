Evidence and updates

Core explanations link to guidelines, labels and systematic reviews. Source type is not the same as certainty. A label warning is not a personal probability or proof of causation.

How we interpret evidence

We distinguish guideline recommendations, trial effects, observational associations and label warnings. We consider population, duration, comparison and outcome, separating glucose changes from prevention of complications. Limited certainty is described in the text.

Original and translations share topic and section IDs. Changes to Korean source content invalidate English and Vietnamese review status and are checked before release. There is no automatic research collection or publishing.

Read the evidence

DPP · Knowler et al. (2002)

In high-risk adults, an intensive lifestyle program reduced diabetes incidence by 58% relative to placebo; metformin reduced it by 31%.

This was a structured program in a high-risk population, not an individual absolute risk reduction or the effect of ordinary walking.

DOI
EMPA-KIDNEY Collaborative Group (2023)

In chronic kidney disease, empagliflozin lowered the composite risk of kidney disease progression or cardiovascular death compared with placebo.

Participants met specific kidney and risk criteria. This does not imply the same effect for every SGLT2 ingredient or every person.

DOI
LEADER · Marso et al. (2016)

Liraglutide reduced major cardiovascular events versus placebo in people with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk.

The finding concerns liraglutide and a high-risk population. Glucose lowering alone does not establish cardiovascular benefit for every medicine.

DOI
SURPASS-2 · Frías et al. (2021)

In a 40-week comparison in metformin-treated type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide reduced HbA1c and weight more than semaglutide 1 mg.

This compares specific doses and duration; it does not prove superiority to every semaglutide dose or superiority for cardiovascular outcomes.
