Living well with blood glucose

This book provides general education about adult prediabetes and type 2 diabetes. It does not diagnose, prescribe or determine individual adverse-event probabilities or doses. Discuss medication changes with your care team.

01 / 09 · Evidence review date 2026-10-04

🩸 Diabetes: what happens inside the body?

Understand glucose, insulin and your test results.

✅ Remember these

  1. Blood glucose is the amount of glucose in your blood. Insulin helps the body use glucose.
  2. Diagnostic thresholds (fasting glucose 126 mg/dL or higher, HbA1c 6.5% or higher) are not your personal target. Set your target with your care team.
  3. Write down whether each test was fasting, after a meal or an oral glucose tolerance test.
Glucose and insulinType 1 and type 2 differDiagnosis is different from treatment targets

Glucose and insulin

Blood glucose is the concentration of glucose in blood. Digestion supplies glucose, and the liver also releases glucose between meals. Insulin from the pancreas reduces liver glucose production and helps muscle and fat cells take up glucose.

Exceptions and cautions

Blood vessel damage is not caused by sticky sugar coating vessels. Prolonged high glucose and other metabolic and vascular factors contribute.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline 대한당뇨병학회 · 2025 당뇨병 진료지침 (2026-02-12 수정 요약)

Type 1 and type 2 differ

Type 1 usually involves autoimmune damage to insulin-producing cells and requires insulin treatment. It can begin in adulthood. Type 2 involves insulin resistance and impaired insulin secretion, influenced by genetics, age, body fat and living conditions.

Exceptions and cautions

Other forms include gestational diabetes and diabetes related to pancreatic disease or medicines. This book focuses on adult prediabetes and type 2, not pregnancy or type 1 treatment.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes: Standards of Care 2026

Diagnosis is different from treatment targets

Laboratory fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL, 2-hour glucose 140–199 mg/dL after a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test, or HbA1c 5.7–6.4% are prediabetes ranges. Diabetes thresholds are 126, 200 and 6.5%, respectively. An oral glucose tolerance test is not an ordinary after-meal reading.

Without unequivocal symptoms or a hyperglycemic crisis, confirmation is usually needed. Anemia and other conditions may affect HbA1c. A clinician interprets the findings together.

An action today

  • Record whether the test was fasting, after a meal or an oral glucose tolerance test.

Exceptions and cautions

An HbA1c below 7% is considered for many adults, but national guidance, age and hypoglycemia risk affect the target. A diagnostic threshold is not automatically your target.

🌱 Prediabetes: begin with achievable changes

Prevention, weight and follow-up tests.

✅ Remember these

  1. Prediabetes is a chance to act. Not everyone with prediabetes develops diabetes.
  2. If you are overweight, work gradually toward losing 5–7% of your weight and about 150 minutes of activity such as walking each week.
  3. Have your glucose checked at least once a year.
Prediabetes is an opportunity to actWeight and activity goalsFollow-up and preventive medication

Prediabetes is an opportunity to act

Prediabetes means glucose is above normal but below diabetes thresholds. Not everyone progresses to diabetes. Review blood pressure, lipids, smoking and daily circumstances as well as glucose.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Prevention or Delay of Diabetes: Standards of Care 2026

Weight and activity goals

Adults at high risk with overweight or obesity can consider individualized weight loss of about 5–7% or more and work toward 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly. With normal weight, frailty or low muscle mass, preserving nutrition and muscle may take priority over weight loss.

An action today

  • Choose one change this week: water instead of sugary drinks, or a ten-minute walk each day.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Prevention or Delay of Diabetes: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Older Adults: Standards of Care 2026

Follow-up and preventive medication

At least annual glucose assessment is recommended for prediabetes, with more frequent testing when risk or changes warrant it. Clinicians may consider metformin for selected high-risk people. Prediabetes does not mean everyone needs medication.

An action today

  • Agree on the next test date and your weight and activity goals at your visit.

Exceptions and cautions

Remission of type 2 is not a cure. It generally means HbA1c below 6.5% for at least three months without glucose-lowering medication; ongoing monitoring remains necessary.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Prevention or Delay of Diabetes: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment: Standards of Care 2026
DPP · Knowler et al. (2002)

In high-risk adults, an intensive lifestyle program reduced diabetes incidence by 58% relative to placebo; metformin reduced it by 31%.

This was a structured program in a high-risk population, not an individual absolute risk reduction or the effect of ordinary walking.

💊 Understand your diabetes medicines

Ingredients, mechanisms, benefits and adverse effects.

✅ Remember these

  1. Take a photo of your medicine bag or prescription, and check the ingredient names next to the product names.
  2. Before starting cold medicine, painkillers, herbal remedies or supplements, tell the pharmacist that you take diabetes medicine.
  3. Do not stop or change medicines yourself when readings improve or when you cannot eat. Ask your care team or pharmacist for a plan in advance.
Why prescriptions differMedicine class mapFormulation, timing and sick days

Why prescriptions differ

Medication choice considers heart failure, cardiovascular and kidney disease, weight, hypoglycemia and cost as well as glucose. Certain SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 medicines have proven heart or kidney benefits in studied populations; those results cannot be assigned equally to every ingredient.

An action today

  • Check ingredient names, strength and whether the formulation is extended-release.
Read the evidence

Formulation, timing and sick days

The same ingredient may have different instructions as immediate-release, extended-release, injectable or oral formulations. Insulin action varies with ingredient, concentration and individual factors. Follow product-specific instructions for medicines such as oral semaglutide.

An action today

  • Ask your pharmacist or clinician for missed-dose and fasting, surgery or vomiting plans in advance.

Exceptions and cautions

Do not apply a blanket continue-or-stop rule when unable to eat or dehydrated. SGLT2 medicines and metformin may need situation-specific changes; stopping basal insulin without guidance in type 1 can be dangerous.

🥗 Food: what and how much to eat?

Learn portions and sustainable choices instead of a forbidden-food list.

✅ Remember these

  1. You do not need to give up rice or noodles. Start with a plate that is half non-starchy vegetables, one quarter protein and one quarter rice or noodles.
  2. Cut down on sugary drinks and fruit juice; eat whole fruit in suitable portions.
  3. Before making big changes to what you eat, agree on medicine and glucose-checking plans with your care team.
Start with a balanced mealCarbohydrate quantity and qualityMeal order is an optional supporting strategyFasting, low-carbohydrate diets and medicines

Start with a balanced meal

You do not have to remove all rice, noodles or bread. Combine nonstarchy vegetables, fish, tofu, beans or lean protein, and appropriate grain portions. The plate method is a starting tool, not a calculation of individual energy needs.

An action today

  • For Korean meals, plan rice portions with fish or tofu and vegetable sides. For Vietnamese meals, consider noodle portions, sweet sauces and drinks together.

Exceptions and cautions

Kidney disease, heart failure or digestive conditions may require changes to protein, sodium, potassium or fluids. More vegetables or water is not always better.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline 대한당뇨병학회 · 2025 당뇨병 진료지침 (2026-02-12 수정 요약)

Carbohydrate quantity and quality

Whole grains, beans and whole fruit can be good choices, but large portions still raise glucose. Juice and sugary drinks make it easy to consume a lot of sugar. Prefer suitable portions of whole fruit; blending alone does not make every food forbidden.

An action today

  • Read serving size, total carbohydrate and sugars together. Sugar-free does not necessarily mean carbohydrate-free.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Meal order is an optional supporting strategy

Some studies find smaller post-meal glucose excursions when vegetables and protein come before carbohydrate. This does not replace portion or food choices; long-term HbA1c effects must be distinguished from short-term glucose changes. Differences in participants and meals prevent a single promised percentage.

Read the evidence
  • Systematic review Efficacy of a meal sequence in patients with type 2 diabetes: systematic review and meta-analysis
  • Systematic review The Impact of Macronutrient Ordering on Postprandial Glycaemic Control in Diabetes: A Systematic Review

Fasting, low-carbohydrate diets and medicines

Several patterns, including Mediterranean-style eating and appropriate carbohydrate reduction, can fit individual preferences and goals. Insulin, sulfonylurea or meglitinide users must consider hypoglycemia when intake falls. Extreme carbohydrate restriction or prolonged fasting with SGLT2 medicines can increase ketoacidosis concerns.

An action today

  • Agree on medication and monitoring plans before major dietary changes.

🌙 Sleep, stress, alcohol and smoking

Build small habits into daily life.

✅ Remember these

  1. Keep regular sleep, such as waking at the same time each day. Mention loud snoring or daytime sleepiness at your visit.
  2. Alcohol can cause low glucose as late as the next day. If you use medicines that can cause low glucose (such as insulin or sulfonylureas), do not drink on an empty stomach.
  3. Glucose is not a grade. Choose one small action for this week, and ask for help to quit smoking if needed.
Review sleep and stressAlcohol can cause delayed hypoglycemiaOne change this week

Review sleep and stress

Sleep duration, quality and regularity, and stress relate to appetite, activity and self-care. Loud snoring and daytime sleepiness warrant assessment for sleep apnea. Treatment does not guarantee improved glucose for everyone.

An action today

  • Keep a regular wake time and choose sustainable relaxation such as comfortable breathing or a short walk.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Alcohol can cause delayed hypoglycemia

Alcohol can interfere with liver glucose supply. Insulin and secretagogue users should consider fasting alcohol intake and hypoglycemia later, including the next day. There is no health reason for a nondrinker to start.

An action today

  • Discuss alcohol, monitoring and hypoglycemia plans, and ask for smoking cessation support.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026

One change this week

Glucose is not a personal grade. Recording meals, medicines, activity and sleep helps you discuss changes at visits. Persistent low mood, anxiety or diabetes distress deserves support.

An action today

  • Choose one small action and one question for your next appointment.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

🚶 Start walking and strength exercise safely

Start small and consider medicines and foot health.

✅ Remember these

  1. Start with a comfortable ten-minute walk after meals, and get up briefly about every 30 minutes when sitting for long periods.
  2. The goal is 150 minutes of moderate activity and strength exercise 2–3 times a week. Increase little by little from where you are now.
  3. If you use medicines that can cause low glucose, carry rapid glucose. Stop right away if you have chest pain, severe dizziness or trouble breathing.
Why activity helpsWeekly goals and a starting doseStrength exercise at homePrepare according to medication risk

Why activity helps

Muscle contraction supports glucose uptake through pathways partly separate from insulin signaling. Regular activity improves insulin sensitivity, fitness and function. Exercise complements food and medication; glucose does not always fall immediately.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Weekly goals and a starting dose

Many adults are advised to aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly and strength exercise on 2–3 nonconsecutive days. Spread aerobic activity over at least three days, with no more than two consecutive inactive days. Begin above your current activity level even if below the target.

An action today

  • Try ten minutes of comfortable walking after a meal, and interrupt long sitting about every 30 minutes.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Strength exercise at home

Choose safe movements such as chair sit-to-stand, wall push-ups, band rows or supported heel raises. Begin with few repetitions and sets, then increase once the movement is stable. Breathe normally and use a stable chair.

Exceptions and cautions

Discuss weight-bearing exercise with an active foot ulcer, and strenuous or straining exercise with proliferative retinopathy. Swimming is not automatically safe for an open foot wound.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Public patient education NIDDK · Diabetes & Foot Problems

Prepare according to medication risk

Insulin, sulfonylurea and meglitinide users should consider glucose before and after activity and delayed hypoglycemia. Pre-exercise carbohydrate needs depend on glucose, active insulin and activity duration. One threshold does not fit everyone.

An action today

  • Carry rapid glucose and agree on a monitoring plan for new activity. Stop and seek help for chest pain, severe dizziness or breathing difficulty.

Exceptions and cautions

Do not try to force glucose down through exercise when unwell with high glucose and ketones.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026

🧭 Low glucose, sick days and monitoring

Actions and visit preparation to review with family.

✅ Remember these

  1. If glucose is below 70 mg/dL and the person is awake and can swallow, take about 15 g of rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes. Repeat if still low.
  2. If the person is unconscious or cannot swallow, give nothing by mouth and call emergency services right away (119 in Korea, 115 in Vietnam).
  3. With severe vomiting, abdominal pain or fast breathing, get care promptly; do not wait based on the glucose number alone.
Low glucose: when awakeImpaired consciousness or seizure is an emergencySick days and ketoacidosisMonitoring, follow-up and visit questions

Low glucose: when awake

Treat glucose below 70 mg/dL as hypoglycemia. An adult who is awake and can swallow can take about 15 g of rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes. Repeat if still low. Use the product label to identify 15 g in glucose tablets or gel.

Exceptions and cautions

With acarbose, use glucose rather than table sugar. Chocolate and high-fat snacks are slower to absorb. One yogurt or a fixed number of candies does not always provide 15 g.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Acarbose prescribing information (US)

Impaired consciousness or seizure is an emergency

Do not give food or drink to someone unconscious or unable to swallow safely. Call emergency services; use prescribed glucagon if available and you have been trained. Severe hypoglycemia means assistance is needed, regardless of the measured glucose.

An action today

  • Share emergency contacts, glucose location and glucagon instructions with family.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026

Sick days and ketoacidosis

Vomiting, dehydration or fasting requires a medicine-specific plan. SGLT2 users can develop ketoacidosis without very high glucose. Severe vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid deep breathing or altered consciousness needs prompt assessment; do not wait based on glucose alone.

Exceptions and cautions

A person with fluid restriction should not be told to drink unlimited water. Plans for type 1 insulin, SGLT2 medicines and metformin differ.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment: Standards of Care 2026
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Empagliflozin / metformin prescribing information (US)
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Sitagliptin / metformin prescribing information (US)

Monitoring, follow-up and visit questions

Wash and dry hands before testing. Count after-meal time from the start of the meal. To convert mg/dL to mmol/L, divide by about 18. Home readings alone do not establish a diagnosis.

HbA1c is usually reviewed every 3–6 months depending on goals and treatment changes. In type 2, kidney function, urine albumin, eyes and feet are assessed from diagnosis with follow-up adjusted to findings. Blood pressure and lipid targets also depend on cardiovascular risk.

An action today

  • Ask: What are my targets? What if glucose goes low? What if I cannot eat? Can I take a new medicine or supplement with these?

🦶 Feet and complications: notice small warning signs

Sensation, circulation, wounds and daily checks.

✅ Remember these

  1. Check the tops, soles and between the toes every day. Use a mirror or ask family to help with the soles.
  2. Even if it does not hurt, get a new wound, blister, redness or swelling checked promptly.
  3. Do not walk barefoot, and avoid hot packs, hot foot baths and removing corns or calluses yourself.
Why can wounds go unnoticed?Daily foot checksWhen to seek careEyes, kidneys and heart matter too

Why can wounds go unnoticed?

Diabetic neuropathy can cause tingling and burning, but also loss of sensation. Poor circulation can make wounds harder to heal. A painless wound is not necessarily safe.

Read the evidence
  • Public patient education NIDDK · Diabetes & Foot Problems

Daily foot checks

Check the tops, soles and between toes for blisters, wounds, redness and swelling. Use a mirror or family help. Wash in lukewarm water, dry between toes, and check inside well-fitting shoes before putting them on.

An action today

  • Avoid barefoot walking; do not cut or chemically remove corns or calluses yourself.

Exceptions and cautions

Hot packs or hot foot baths can burn feet with reduced sensation.

Read the evidence
  • Public patient education NIDDK · Diabetes & Foot Problems

When to seek care

Report a new wound, blister, redness, warmth, swelling or drainage promptly. Fever, spreading redness, black discoloration or a very cold or discolored foot requires urgent assessment. Do not delay because it is painless.

Read the evidence
  • Public patient education NIDDK · Diabetes & Foot Problems

Eyes, kidneys and heart matter too

Eye and kidney complications can be silent early, so follow-up testing matters. Manage glucose, blood pressure, lipids, smoking and appropriate medicines together. Tingling can also have causes such as B12 deficiency and needs assessment.

🔎 Supplements: separate evidence from promises

Glucose lowering, deficiency correction and nerve symptoms are different goals.

✅ Remember these

  1. Supplements do not replace diabetes medicines.
  2. Avoid advertisements that promise a cure or say you can stop your medicines.
  3. Show the ingredients and strengths of products you take or plan to take at your visit, especially if you have kidney problems.
Quality and applicability, not just study countCorrecting deficiency is different from treating glucoseAlpha-lipoic acid: limited long-term benefitBerberine, cinnamon, banaba and omega-3

Quality and applicability, not just study count

A meta-analysis can include small, biased or short studies. A change in glucose does not prove prevention of complications. Trial formulations and doses may differ from products sold. Routine supplements for glucose management are not generally recommended.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026

Correcting deficiency is different from treating glucose

Long-term metformin use is associated with lower B12. Discuss testing with anemia, tingling or prolonged use. B12, vitamin D or magnesium supplementation should consider deficiency and individual factors; glucose improvement is not guaranteed for everyone.

Exceptions and cautions

Some minerals can accumulate with impaired kidney function. Avoid stacking products without assessment.

Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Prevention or Delay of Diabetes: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • US prescribing information DailyMed · Sitagliptin / metformin prescribing information (US)

Alpha-lipoic acid: limited long-term benefit

Short-term symptom findings should be separated from long-term benefit. A 2024 Cochrane review found probably little or no improvement in neuropathy symptoms after six months or more. It should not be presented as proven nerve protection.

Read the evidence
  • Systematic review Baicus et al. · Alpha-lipoic acid for diabetic peripheral neuropathy · Cochrane

Berberine, cinnamon, banaba and omega-3

Even with glucose-lowering studies, certainty, product quality and interactions need separate review. Berberine and similar supplements should not replace diabetes medicines. Cardiovascular findings for specific prescription products are not the same as findings for ordinary omega-3 supplements.

An action today

  • Bring product, ingredient and strength details to visits; avoid advertisements promising cures or stopping medication.
Read the evidence
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being: Standards of Care 2026
  • Clinical guideline ADA · Cardiovascular Disease and Risk Management: Standards of Care 2026

Evidence and updates

Core explanations link to guidelines, labels and systematic reviews. Source type is not the same as certainty. A label warning is not a personal probability or proof of causation.

How we interpret evidence

We distinguish guideline recommendations, trial effects, observational associations and label warnings. We consider population, duration, comparison and outcome, separating glucose changes from prevention of complications. Limited certainty is described in the text.

Original and translations share topic and section IDs. Changes to Korean source content invalidate English and Vietnamese review status and are checked before release. There is no automatic research collection or publishing.
